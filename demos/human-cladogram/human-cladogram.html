Humans on the Tree of Life

A schematic cladogram. The purpose of this figure is to show that Homo sapiens is one branch within a much larger evolutionary tree, not something apart from it.

Branch lengths are not proportional to time. Many lineages are omitted for clarity. Use the buttons below to trace the lineage leading to modern humans.

For lecture use, step through the tree first, then finish by highlighting the full path to Homo sapiens. Arrow keys (or a presenter remote) also step forward and back.

How to read the tree

  • Every branch point represents a lineage split from a common ancestor.
  • Terminal labels are the named taxa or representative lineages shown in this simplified tree.
  • Left–right position is just layout. It does not imply progress or superiority.
  • The aim is to see where humans are nested within the tree, not to memorize every displayed branch.
This is a teaching figure, not a complete phylogeny. It emphasizes the logic of branching descent and the placement of humans within that tree.

Selected Linnaean ranks

  • Animalia — kingdom
  • Chordata — phylum
  • Mammalia — class
  • Primates — order
  • Hominidae — family
  • Homo — genus

The tree itself shows branching relationships; the rank labels are there only to help orient students.
