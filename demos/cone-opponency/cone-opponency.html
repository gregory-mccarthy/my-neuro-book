What is idealised here. Spectral sensitivities are Govardovskii A1 nomograms (λmax 420, 534, 564 nm), which are already in quantal units — the right units when you are counting photons. They omit lens and macular filtering, so they sit close to but not on the Stockman–Sharpe cone fundamentals. Absorption probability is set to the normalized sensitivity, so a photon at λmax is always caught; real quantal efficiency is far lower. Counts are per cone, not per class, so the mosaic proportions do not masquerade as sensitivity. The 21-cone row has 12 L, 7 M and 2 S cones; the S cones are sparse and spaced apart, as in a real mosaic. The L:M estimate in the absorption stage is shown over a fixed five-second integration window, a slowed-down stand-in for the few tens of milliseconds a cone actually integrates over, alongside the all-time estimate that no real eye has access to. On the photon-to-spike stage, cone voltage is a compressive saturating function of absorptions; univariance is untouched by this, since equal absorptions still give equal responses. The bipolar row is the single-cone center. The surround is drawn as a horizontal-cell pool that responds instantly; real surrounds are slower and also involve amacrine input. The ganglion cell's drive is the bipolar center minus that surround, both read from the traces on screen. The maintained rate of 22 Hz and all gains are schematic; real midget cells vary widely. The metamer swatches are drawn from each side's own three quantal catches through the display's primaries; they are not colorimetry, and two sides with the same catches necessarily get the same swatch. Primaries with negative weights are moved to the test side, as in the classic color-matching experiments. The L–M correlation on the last panel uses this demo's own stimuli: narrowband patches, broadband increments, and smooth synthetic reflectances for twelve natural-looking surfaces under three illuminants. Treat the numbers as an illustration; the measurement from natural images is Ruderman, Cronin and Chiao (1998).
