What is idealised here. Spectral sensitivities are Govardovskii A1 nomograms (λmax 420, 534, 564 nm), which are already in quantal units — the right units when you are counting photons, and a step people often skip. They omit lens and macular filtering, so they sit close to but not on the Stockman–Sharpe cone fundamentals. Absorption probability is set to the normalized sensitivity, so a photon at λmax is always caught; real quantal efficiency is far lower and depends on outer-segment optical density. Counts are per cone, not per class, so the L:M:S mosaic ratio does not masquerade as sensitivity. Spike rasters begin at the ganglion cell, where spikes actually exist; the cones show graded absorption counts only. Channel weights are schematic, as in the chapter. The L–M correlation on the last panel is computed over this demo's own stimulus set — a uniform sweep of narrowband patches plus broadband increments — so treat the number as an illustration of why the axes are rotated, not as a measurement from natural scenes. On the four-cell chain, the cone's voltage follows a compressive saturating function of absorptions, which is why a doubled flash does not double the deflection; univariance is untouched by this, since equal absorptions still give equal responses. The bipolar row is drawn from its single-cone center only — a real midget bipolar also has a surround — and its larger swing reflects genuine gain at the first synapse. The maintained rate of 22 Hz and the channel weights are schematic; real midget cells vary widely, and the center/surround toggle shows that the same opponency arises from a single-cone center against a mixed surround, with no cone-selective wiring. The metamer swatch is the display's rendering of the solved primary mix, not an independent colorimetric calculation; the point is that both sides land on the same swatch, not the exact hue. The reflectance spectra on the last panel are smooth approximations to natural surfaces, not measured data.
