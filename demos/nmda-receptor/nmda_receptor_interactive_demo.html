Reading the ion animation
Each moving circle is one ion crossing the pore. The rate of ions is set by conductance multiplied by that ion's driving force, so ions of different species move at different rates and can move in different directions at the same voltage.

Predict before you look

With the current settings, will Ca²⁺ enter the spine through NMDA receptors at a meaningful rate?

What to notice

At negative membrane potentials, glutamate can bind the NMDA receptor while Mg²⁺ still obstructs the pore. Ligand binding and membrane voltage are separate requirements.

Which direction a synapse moves depends on the amount and time course of the calcium signal, not simply on whether NMDA receptors opened. Weak activity that produces a small, drawn-out calcium rise can weaken a synapse that strong activity would strengthen. What this model does and does not do
  • NMDA receptors need glutamate and a co-agonist, glycine or D-serine depending on the synapse and brain region. Both sites must be occupied.
  • Mg²⁺ block follows 1 / (1 + [Mg]/3.57 · e^(−0.062V)), the standard empirical description. It is graded, and its voltage dependence shifts with Mg²⁺ concentration.
  • Mg²⁺ occupancy is drawn as flicker rather than as an ion being pushed out of the pore. The ion binds and unbinds rapidly; depolarization changes how much of the time it is bound.
  • Current is conductance multiplied by driving force. Net current through both receptors reverses near 0 mV. Ca²⁺ influx does not, because the calcium equilibrium potential is far more positive.
  • AMPA and NMDA conductances use single-exponential deactivation with very different time constants. Real receptors have multiple kinetic states, desensitization, and subunit-dependent kinetics.
  • When the voltage is not held, one synapse is given enough conductance to depolarize the whole compartment. A real single synapse produces a fraction of a millivolt at the soma.
  • Currents are in arbitrary units. Concentrations, single-channel conductance, receptor numbers, spine geometry, calcium buffering, and stochastic gating are all omitted.
  • The plasticity panel is a compressed illustration of the calcium-level account. Real plasticity involves many interacting pathways, other calcium sources including voltage-gated channels and internal stores, and depends on receptor subunit composition.
