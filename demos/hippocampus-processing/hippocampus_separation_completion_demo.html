About this simulation

A deliberately small network for building intuition, after David Marr's 1971 theory of the hippocampus and its later development by Treves, Rolls, McNaughton and others. It is not a realistic model.

  • Cells: 200 entorhinal (EC), 1,000 dentate gyrus (DG), 400 CA3. Each cell is either on or off. In every population a fixed number fire (20 by default), standing in for inhibition. The grids are for display only: neighboring dots mean nothing.
  • Wiring: each DG cell receives 40 random EC inputs; each CA3 cell receives 20 random DG inputs (mossy fibers) and 60 random EC inputs (direct perforant path). Every CA3 cell is connected to every other.
  • Storing a memory: the mossy fibers choose which CA3 cells fire. Connections between CA3 cells that fire together strengthen, those between firing and silent cells weaken, and the direct-path synapses from the active EC cells onto the chosen CA3 cells strengthen.
  • Recall: a cue drives CA3 through one road. Then, one swap at a time, the active cell with the least support from the other active cells switches off and the silent cell with the most support switches on, as long as the swap lowers the network's energy.
  • Left out: CA1, time and theta rhythm, inhibitory interneurons as cells, mossy cells, new neurons, spikes, the real (sparse, about 1%) CA3 wiring and the real cell numbers. All numbers on screen are properties of this toy network, not measurements from brains.

The companion guide explains the science, every control and the model's equations.
