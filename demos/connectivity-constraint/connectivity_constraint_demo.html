What the student should notice

1. Dense connectivity hits a wall Neurons increase linearly, but all pairwise connections increase quadratically.
2. Modularity changes the scaling rule Most connections stay local; a small number of long-range bridges carry information farther.
3. Bigger brains must economize on wire Real brains use sparse connectivity, local circuits, hierarchical organization, and limited long-range axons rather than all-to-all wiring.
Human-scale thought experiment
≈ 77 lb
The lecture analogy: retaining much denser, rodent-like wiring as neuron number scales upward would impose an enormous wiring-volume burden. The simulation is conceptual, not a literal anatomical mass calculator.
Scientific note: no mammalian brain is all-to-all. Dense mode is a deliberately exaggerated mathematical demonstration of the connectivity problem.
Teaching move: let dense mode fail first; then switch modes without resetting.
