Center–surround receptive fields

A ganglion cell does not report how much light falls on it. It reports how the light in one small region compares with the light around it. Everything below is recorded the way a physiologist would: flash a stimulus, count the spikes, repeat.

What the center–surround model predicts

Expected sustained response of a whole sheet of these cells, warm above the maintained rate and cool below. This panel is a model prediction, not a recording.

Speed Arrow keys step through the demonstrations; space pauses. Sound plays at 1× speed only.
What is idealized here. Space: the receptive field is a difference of two concentric two-dimensional Gaussians, each normalized to unit volume, with the surround weighted by the strength control. The surround is made stronger per unit area than in most real cells, so that a single flashed spot can reveal it; real surrounds are usually wider and weaker. Time: center and surround signals are smoothed with time constants of 10 and 18 ms, so the surround lags slightly, and a transient stage lets 70% of any step response decay with a 150 ms time constant. That is what produces onset bursts, the burst when a suppressing light goes off, and the brief burst a uniform field gives. Output: the rate is a maintained 20 spikes/s plus the filtered signal, floored at zero and compressed toward a 160 spikes/s ceiling; spikes are drawn at random from that rate, with a 2 ms refractory period. All of this is a descriptive model, not a circuit: real surrounds are assembled by horizontal and amacrine pathways and are not perfectly circular. The cell sums light linearly, so it cannot show the nonlinear subunit behaviour of Y-type cells. Spots and annuli are full-contrast increments; the edge stimuli run at 40% contrast and the grating at 20%, drifting at 1 cycle per second, to keep the cells away from their floor and ceiling. On the Hermann grid only the horizontal streets bend, a simplified version of Geier and colleagues' demonstration; that panel shows the model's expected sustained rates rather than recorded spikes. The sign reversal with center size is the cleaner failure there, since it needs no assumption about the bent-line percept.
