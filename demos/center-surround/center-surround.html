Center–surround receptive fields

A ganglion cell does not report how much light falls on it. It reports how the light in one small region compares with the light around it. Each demonstration below uses the same cell.

What is idealized here. The receptive field is a difference of two concentric two-dimensional Gaussians, each normalized to unit volume, with the surround weighted by the strength control. This is the standard descriptive model, not a circuit: real surrounds are assembled by horizontal and amacrine pathways, are not perfectly circular, and are not perfectly balanced. Responses here are sustained; the transient bursts many cells produce at stimulus onset and offset are omitted, which is why a uniform field looks quieter here than it would on a real electrode. The cell is linear by construction, so it cannot show the rectified, nonlinear subunit behaviour of Y-type cells. Spatial scale is given in degrees of visual angle for a field four degrees across; center sizes of real primate ganglion cells vary by more than an order of magnitude with eccentricity. On the Hermann grid, only the horizontal streets bend, so the intersections remain proper crossings; this is a simplified version of Geier and colleagues' sinusoidal-line demonstration, and the model's predicted suppression weakens somewhat with curvature rather than staying fixed. The sign reversal with center size is the cleaner failure, since it needs no assumption about the bent-line percept.
