1. THE DISTINCT WORK OF EACH CHAPTER

THE ORGAN OF TOMORROW
Question: Why must absent, hidden, or possible conditions influence present
control, and how do frontal systems participate in that capacity?
Responsibility, now established by the author: provide the unit's substantial
anatomical and functional-anatomical foundation. Explain frontal geography,
regional and connectional differences, the routes by which bodily state and
motivationally relevant information influence frontal systems, recurrent
interactions with basal ganglia, thalamus, and dopaminergic systems, and the
routes by which frontal activity changes action and bodily regulation. Use
several anatomical figures. Organize this material around evolutionary
continuity and the author's control-theoretic interpretation, following the
explanatory pattern of the Unit 2 hypothalamus overview. Use selected examples
to establish the problem; reserve extended experimental and clinical analyses
for the focused chapters.

A COMMON CURRENCY
Question: How can unlike expected outcomes influence one choice while their
identities and relations to bodily state remain available for revision?
Responsibility: behavioral equivalence, value and identity coding, causal
perturbation, selective revaluation, and outcome inference. Connect the bodily
foundation to the current comparison without reteaching all the physiology.
Keep goods-specific offer-value responses distinct from the more general
chosen-value signal.

HOLDING THE LINE
Question: How does selected information remain effective across delay,
interference, movement, and changing circumstances?
Responsibility: selective persistence and appropriate revision, distributed
representations, persistent/changing/latent activity, recurrent circuits, and
clinical failures of maintained control. Connect these states explicitly to
the action machinery previously introduced in Unit 5.

FEELING THE FUTURE
Question: How does a represented consequence change the organism before it
occurs, and how does bodily information participate in ongoing choice?
Responsibility: prospective bodily preparation, interoception, autonomic
measurement, the body-loop/as-if-loop distinction, and the evidence and limits
of the somatic-marker hypothesis. Deepen embodiment already present in the
preceding chapters. Clarify the relevant incoming and returning pathways.

THE FUTURES OF OTHERS
Question: How do another agent's knowledge, goals, reactions, and possible
outcomes influence the organism's present behavior?
Responsibility: social learning, concern, intention-outcome integration,
developmental contrasts, and the distinction between knowing a norm and using
it during interaction. Establish the boundary with the fuller treatment of
the social niche in Unit 8 after its current text is checked.

WANDERING WHEN FULL
Question: When is acquiring information worth its present costs, and how does
the animal regulate exploration relative to familiar courses of action?
Responsibility: information value, directed and random exploration, state,
horizon, sampling, and the implications for future controllability. Build on
Unit 2's already-developed exploration argument and circuitry. Explain the
additional control problem rather than presenting that foundation as new.

TOMORROW NEVER COMES
Question: How does an available and potentially valuable goal recruit an
initiated and sustained course of action?
Responsibility: prompting, preserved capacities, effort and vigor, initiation,
and the distinctions among apathy, abulia, auto-activation deficit, and
akinetic mutism. Make the connections to previously described action systems
clear and provide the unit's final handoff to predictive maps and memory.

These are responsibilities for explanation. They do not assign six operations
to isolated brain regions or describe six completed stages of one decision.

2. REPETITION: WHAT SHOULD RECUR AND WHAT CAN BE SHORTENED

Useful recurrence applies an established idea to a different mechanism,
measurement, or dissociation. Redundant recurrence repeats its definition or
general defense without adding explanatory work.

A. Shared network inventories
The later chapters repeatedly rebuild a broad account involving orbital and
medial frontal cortex, amygdala, insula, hippocampus, hypothalamus, striatum,
thalamus, and brainstem. Examples occur in Feeling the Future, "Prospective
valuation belongs to a distributed loop"; The Futures of Others, "Prospective
social control belongs to a distributed loop"; Wandering When Full, "The
machinery is distributed and evolutionarily old"; and Tomorrow Never Comes,
"A distributed initiation system."
Proposed treatment: establish the shared setting in the overview. Later
chapters should recall it briefly and explain the particular connections that
do work in that chapter. Preserve their concrete circuit and intervention
examples. Demonstrating the recurrent account through mechanisms can reduce
the repeated general defense of that account.

B. Overview versus focused experimental narratives
The overview already discusses remembered/forgotten stimuli, delayed saccades,
gambling and autonomic findings, goal neglect, perseveration, utilization
behavior, and impaired initiation. These examples recur in focused chapters.
Proposed treatment: use the overview's examples to orient and compare control
problems; give detailed procedures, measurements, and limitations their main
home in the focused chapter. Decide the degree of compression with the author.

C. Distinct forms of "knowing is not enough"
Feeling the Future distinguishes reportable knowledge from its prospective
influence on choice. The Futures of Others distinguishes recognizing a social
violation afterward from regulating the interaction as it unfolds. Tomorrow
Never Comes examines access to action through external prompting.
Proposed treatment: retain the different empirical contrasts. Briefly recall
the shared principle and emphasize what each new dissociation reveals.

D. The exploration-to-initiation handoff
The final discussion in Wandering When Full and the opening of Tomorrow Never
Comes repeat the sequence in which exploration makes an option available,
valuation makes it desirable, bodily change makes its consequences felt, and
maintenance keeps it effective without guaranteeing action.
Proposed treatment: retain one complete recap and make the adjacent passage a
short, direct transition into initiation.

E. Local explanatory duplication
The Futures of Others defines several social/affective terms in its opening
prose and repeats them in the immediately following "Empathy is not one thing"
callout. Preserve the distinctions; decide whether the callout should become
a genuinely compact reference aid during that chapter's later editing pass.

4. ANATOMY: THE CONNECTIONS THE STUDENT SHOULD BE ABLE TO FOLLOW

Anatomy is already present, but its explanatory depth varies. Feeling the
Future names uncinate and amygdalofugal pathways. Wandering When Full describes
specific prelimbic–zona-incerta–periaqueductal relationships. Tomorrow Never
Comes discusses medial frontal motor territories and pallidal/thalamic
contributions. Other passages mainly name systems and their general roles.

The pass should identify where students can follow a connection and where
they are asked to accept that two functions interact. For each central example:

1. What evidence about bodily state or an expected outcome is available?
2. Which identified routes allow that evidence to influence the relevant
   frontal and connected systems?
3. Which connections allow the resulting change in network activity to alter
   attention, action selection, preparation, persistence, or movement?
4. How do bodily and sensory consequences return to modify ongoing control?

For each proposed anatomical explanation, record the source, the species and
task when relevant, and whether the relationship is a direct projection,
influence through intervening structures, or a functional association. This
is the basis for later text and diagrams. A regional activity correlation
alone should not be converted into an anatomical projection.

Author decision: the overview supplies a substantial shared anatomical
account and several anatomical figures. Each focused chapter develops a
small number of connections that explain its particular evidence. Earlier
chapters supply the mechanisms they have already established, with a brief
reminder at the point of use.

4A. THE UNIT 2 OVERVIEW AS THE MODEL

Source: overview-hypothalamus(20261020-022809).qmd, supplied by the author
for this comparison. Its organizational sequence is:

- The adaptive/control problem in "Homeostasis is not enough."
- Evolutionary continuity in "An ancient and conserved organ."
- Anatomical organization, a sagittal figure, and a compact nucleus/role table.
- Incoming information in "Reading the body: the sensory interface."
- Outgoing influences in "Acting on the body: the effector interface."
- The organizing interpretation revisited through the machinery just described.
- A short account of methods, species, and what the evidence establishes.
- "Boxes within boxes": explicit explanatory boundaries and an argument for
  the progression of the subsequent chapters.

This is a model for explanatory organization and authorial presence; adopting
it does not require repeating every heading, example, or simplification. The
new overview should preserve deliberate statements of the author's perspective,
including first-person judgments where useful. Reducing incidental asides
should not erase the voice that explains why the book takes this perspective.

The Unit 2 ending is especially consequential. It already extends regulation
from immediate internal variables to operating states, safety, reproduction,
social relationships, and future controllability through exploration. Unit 6
must build on that development. It should make explicit what frontal circuitry
adds when remembered, inferred, absent, and possible conditions govern present
action. Prediction, motivation, and future controllability are already part
of the book's foundation.

PROPOSED OVERVIEW SEQUENCE

1. The control problem and the author's claim
   Retain the title The Organ of Tomorrow and the problem of what governs
   action when the present scene does not specify it. Begin from the regulated
   body and predictive action systems that the book has already established.
   Working thesis for discussion: frontal systems extend regulation by making
   remembered, inferred, and possible conditions effective in present control.

2. Ancient control problems and differentiated frontal systems
   Keep the evolutionary argument. Explain what is conserved and what varies
   across the species used in the unit. A mouse circuit and a human frontal
   parcel require an explicit comparison, not an assumed one-to-one identity.

3. How frontal cortex is organized
   Establish lateral, medial, and orbital geography; the relationship of PFC
   to neighboring motor territories; cortical differentiation; and meaningful
   regional connectional biases. Pair location with the kinds of information
   and targets available to an area. Avoid assigning one psychological faculty
   to each colored territory.

4. How bodily state and the world influence frontal circuits
   Reconnect to the already-taught sensors and regulatory systems. Distinguish
   peripheral chemical and neural signals, hypothalamic/brainstem state,
   sensory cues, learned outcomes, and contextual information. Explain selected
   routes through insular, amygdalar, thalamic, and other connected systems.
   Include identified direct hypothalamic projections where relevant, while
   distinguishing their anatomical existence from the particular information
   they have been shown to carry.
   Include hunger, thirst, protection/shelter, and reproductive/social motives
   as requested. Their physiological and environmental determinants should
   be described specifically; no common dedicated drive-to-PFC cable is
   assumed. Use a few well-supported routes instead of an exhaustive inventory.

5. Recurrent interactions with basal ganglia, thalamus, and dopamine
   Build on the verified accounts in @sec-basal-ganglia and
   @sec-what-its-worth. Canonical pathways, graded/convergent striatal
   organization, output targets, differentiated dopamine signals, and the
   ascending spiral have already been taught. Explain particular frontal
   entry and return relationships, selected thalamic interactions, and their
   relevance to the unit's control problems. Give cortical dopamine
   identifiable sources, targets, and effects, distinguishing this account
   from the striatal modulation and plasticity already established.

6. How frontal activity changes action and the body
   Trace selected cortical routes toward premotor, supplementary/cingulate
   motor, and other action systems; subcortical routes through basal ganglia;
   and influences on hypothalamic and brainstem regulation. Connect these to
   the descending machinery already taught. Include the return of sensory
   and bodily consequences so that inputs and outputs remain limbs of
   continuing interaction rather than completed stages of a single decision.

7. Evidence, explanatory boundaries, and the six problems ahead
   Give a short methods/species account, with optional deeper material in
   callouts. State what the overview has opened and what each later chapter
   will explain. Introduce the six interacting control problems and hand
   directly into Common Currency.

SPECIFIC EVIDENCE REPAIRS

- The body-loop/as-if-loop distinction is identified as a theoretical account.
  Universal dependence on a fresh peripheral response is not attributed to
  Damasio. Five questions now separate elicitation, covariation, intervention,
  task-specific necessity, and temporal precedence over explicit knowledge.
- Autonomic output, interoception, affect, conscious feeling, and value remain
  distinct. Electrodermal activity measures one sympathetic effector system,
  not total autonomic state, negative value, or a complete somatic marker.
- Cranial IX/X afferents to NTS and caudal NTS projections are grounded in
  primate tracing. No unbroken NTS-to-vmPFC chain is claimed. Cortical fMRI
  evidence is kept separate from anatomical tracing.
- Garfinkel's cardiac-phase experiment concerns fearful-face detection and
  intensity judgments in separate tasks. It supports bodily-state influences
  on threat processing, not a selective afferent manipulation or proof of
  peripheral necessity in economic choice.
- Amygdala and ventromedial lesion groups are distinguished by their actual
  anticipatory, obtained-outcome, and conditioning measures. Neither group
  is equated with removal of one theoretical loop or all autonomic capacity.
- Folloni's uncinate/amygdalofugal evidence is described as diffusion
  tractography in humans and macaques, not proof of direction or synapses.
- The gambling schedule distinguishes nominal gain, penalty frequency,
  total deductions, and net return. A penalty need not be a net losing trial.
  Ten-card schedule totals are not arbitrary ten-trial guarantees.
- Pre-selection skin conductance is not necessarily earlier than internal
  commitment. Previous-outcome activity and slow response timing matter.
- Maia–McClelland measured reportable knowledge and behavior, not simultaneous
  skin conductance. Their results weaken an inference of absent knowledge.
